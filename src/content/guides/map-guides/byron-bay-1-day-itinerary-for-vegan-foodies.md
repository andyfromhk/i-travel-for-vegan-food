Spend a Sunday in Byron Bay the way the locals do. On the first Sunday of every month, you'll find the Byron Community Market on the Main Beach. It is Byron's original market since 1987, with hundreds of stalls selling handmade clothing, jewellery, homewares, art and takeaway food, plus live music by the sea.

This itinerary builds a full day in Byron Bay on the market day. For travellers visiting not on the market day, you can skip that part of the itinerary or replacing it with a relaxing morning at the main beach.

Bring comfortable shoes, water, a hat and sunscreen for the lighthouse walk, and a tote bag for your market finds. Our starting point is Combi on Fletcher Street, just a couple of minutes from Main Beach.

---

## Combi

{{place: combi}}

![](/media/guides/byron-bay-1-day-itinerary-for-vegan-foodies/byron-bay-vegan-itinerary-combi.webp)

Kick off your market day with brekkie at [Combi](/restaurants/combi) on Fletcher Street. It's one of Byron Bay's best-known brunch cafes, with an organic menu of açaí bowls, smoothies, toasts and specialty coffee, and good vegan options across the menu. It opens at 7am every day, so you can eat before the market crowds arrive.

Don't miss the loaded banana bread. I tried my friend's and it was a highlight: toasted with crispy edges while still soft in the middle, then loaded with vegan cream and fruit. The mocha I had, however, was a bit watery, so you might want to go for a smoothie instead.

{{route: 5-minute walk | Walk 5 minutes to the Main Beach foreshore.}}

## Byron Community Market

{{place: -28.6422055, 153.6162646 | Main Beach}}

![](/media/guides/byron-bay-1-day-itinerary-for-vegan-foodies/byron-bay-vegan-itinerary-market.webp)

Next, head to the Byron Community Market on the Main Beach foreshore. Running since 1987, it's Byron's original market, held on the first Sunday of every month from 8am until early afternoon (with extra markets on the third Sunday in December and January).

With more than 200 stalls, it's easy to spend a couple of hours here. You'll find handmade fashion, jewellery, homewares, gifts and original artwork, plus live music from local musicians, all with the ocean as your backdrop. There are food stalls too, but they change from month to month, so ask about vegan options on the day. I'd save your appetite for lunch, which is just a short walk away.

> **Tip**: There's also the Byron Farmers Market on Thursday 7-11am if you couldn't plan for this monthly Sunday market.

{{route: 8-minute walk | Walk about 8 minutes into town along Jonson Street.}}

## Fundies Organics

{{place: fundies-organics}}

![](/media/guides/byron-bay-1-day-itinerary-for-vegan-foodies/byron-bay-vegan-itinerary-fundies.webp)

Time for lunch! [Fundies Organics](/restaurants/fundies-organics) is a long-running health food and organic grocery store on Byron's main street. It also has a separate garden cafe and fresh food section with vegan options like salads, burgers, brownies and more, and it's open on Sundays, so it's an easy lunch stop after the market. I haven't tried the cafe food myself yet, so check which dishes are vegan when you order.

Before you leave, have a browse through the store. It's a good place to stock up on vegan snacks, plant milks and organic produce for the rest of your stay.

> **Tip**: If you're in Byron on a weekday, [Manna Haven](/restaurants/manna-haven) is a great all-vegan lunch alternative a few minutes down Jonson Street. It's a not-for-profit cafe with a changing counter of hot and cold dishes, but it's only open for weekday lunches, so it's closed on market day.

{{route: 5-minute walk | Walk 5 minutes south along Jonson Street.}}

## Red Ginger

{{place: red-ginger}}

![](/media/guides/byron-bay-1-day-itinerary-for-vegan-foodies/byron-bay-vegan-itinerary-red-ginger.webp)

Just down the road, [Red Ginger](/restaurants/red-ginger) has been a Byron Bay favourite since 2002. It's part Asian grocery store, part dumpling house and part gift shop. It's a beautiful store to wander through, with handmade gifts, ceramics and teaware alongside shelves of Asian groceries, so it's a great place to pick up a souvenir if the market didn't have what you were after.

Check out the hot food cupboard at the front of the shop, which has quite a few vegan options that are easy to take away as a snack for the walk. The red bean bun and soy skin spring roll were tasty. The skin on the gyoza was a bit dry, but that's to be expected when it had been sitting in the cupboard for a while. Red Ginger also sells meat dishes, so check the labels or ask the staff which items are vegan.

{{route: 35-minute walk along the beach | Walk 8 minutes north along Jonson Street to Main Beach, then head east along the sand for about 25 minutes to The Pass.}}

## Main Beach & The Pass

{{place: -28.6371805, 153.6282804 | Cape Byron}}

![](/media/guides/byron-bay-1-day-itinerary-for-vegan-foodies/byron-bay-vegan-itinerary-whales.webp)

Now it's time to walk off lunch. From Main Beach, head east along the sand past Clarkes Beach until you reach The Pass, Byron's best-known surf break. There are usually surfers out all day, so it's a fun spot to stop and watch them catch the waves rolling around the point. If you're lucky, you may be able to spot some whales as well.

The Pass is also where the Walgun Cape Byron walking track starts to climb towards the lighthouse. The full track is a 3.7km loop through rainforest, beaches, grassland and clifftops, and you'll want about two hours to enjoy it properly with stops along the way.

{{route: 40-minute walk uphill | Follow the Walgun Cape Byron walking track uphill from The Pass, past Wategos Beach and the Most Easterly Point, to the lighthouse. It takes about 40 minutes, with some stairs along the way.}}

## Cape Byron Lighthouse

{{place: -28.638465, 153.6362461 | Cape Byron}}

![](/media/guides/byron-bay-1-day-itinerary-for-vegan-foodies/byron-bay-vegan-itinerary-lighthouse.webp)

At the top of the climb, you'll reach Cape Byron Lighthouse ([*address*](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1\&query=Cape%20Byron%20Lighthouse\&query_place_id=ChIJD5SVBNFjkGsRKDWDW1Fahz0)), Byron Bay's most famous landmark. Completed in 1901, it sits on the most easterly point of mainland Australia, with sweeping views up and down the coast and out over the ocean. Bring water and a hat, as the afternoon sun can be strong up here. The lighthouse grounds are open until sunset.

{{route: 45-minute walk back to town | Continue on the loop down the Tallow Ridge track to Lighthouse Road, then follow it back into town to Fletcher Street. It takes about 45 minutes, or a taxi or rideshare is a quick alternative if your legs are tired.}}

## Frankies Gelato

{{place: frankies-gelato}}

![](/media/guides/byron-bay-1-day-itinerary-for-vegan-foodies/byron-bay-vegan-itinerary-frankies-gelato.webp)

After the walk, you've earned a scoop at [Frankies Gelato](/restaurants/frankies-gelato), a Sicilian-inspired gelateria a short stroll from Main Beach. Everything is churned on site with local ingredients, and around 40% of the flavours are vegan (marked "VGN" on the menu), so there's always a good selection to choose from.

The gelato here is creamy and delicious. I tried the matcha macadamia flavour, which had a great balance of nutty macadamia and strong matcha. You can also have your gelato in a Sicilian-style brioche bun, but brioche usually contains egg and butter, so check with the staff before ordering it. Frankies stays open late, so you can always come back for a second scoop after dinner.

{{route: 2-minute walk | Walk 2 minutes along Bay Street to the top of Jonson Street.}}

## Byron Bay Town Centre

{{place: -28.6413416, 153.6130038 | Town Centre}}

![](/media/guides/byron-bay-1-day-itinerary-for-vegan-foodies/byron-bay-vegan-itinerary.webp)

With your gelato in hand, spend the late afternoon wandering Byron Bay's compact town centre. Start at the top of Jonson Street, the main street, by the Beach Hotel, the iconic pub right across the road from Main Beach. From here, stroll south along Jonson Street and the side streets around Lawson and Byron Streets, which are lined with surf shops, fashion boutiques, homewares stores and cafes. Some shops close early on Sundays, so don't leave it too late.

{{route: 5-minute walk | Walk back to Fletcher Street for dinner. No Bones is just a few doors from Frankies.}}

## No Bones

{{place: no-bones}}

![](/media/guides/byron-bay-1-day-itinerary-for-vegan-foodies/byron-bay-vegan-itinerary-no-bones.webp)

End your market day with an all-vegan dinner at [No Bones](/restaurants/no-bones), just a short walk from Main Beach. It's Byron Bay's go-to spot for vegan food and cocktails, with a slightly fancy feel and smaller portions, which makes it a lovely way to celebrate the end of your day (and a great pick for a date night).

In my opinion, the desserts are even stronger than the savoury dishes. The tiramisu is the standout, and I think it's one of the best tiramisus I've ever had, so make sure you save room for it. On the savoury side, the carbonara has a good flavour overall, though I was expecting the 'vegan egg yolk' on top to be runny. The sticky tofu comes with a sauce that was a bit too bitter for my taste. The service, however, was amazing from start to finish.

> **Tip**: No Bones is dinner only and opens from 5pm on Sundays. Book ahead, as weekends get busy, especially on market days.
