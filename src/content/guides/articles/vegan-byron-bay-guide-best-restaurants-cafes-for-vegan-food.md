You may think of Byron Bay as a place for beaches, surfing and the lighthouse walk. But this laid-back, health-conscious town is also a great place to eat vegan, with plant-based options at most cafes and a few fully vegan spots worth planning a meal around.

In this Byron Bay guide, let me show you my favourite vegan spots from my recent trip, from an all-vegan dinner with one of the best tiramisus I've had, to matcha gelato and an Asian grocery store with a hot food cupboard. I've also added a few popular vegan places that I haven't visited yet (clearly marked), so you have even more options to choose from.

## Must-Visit Byron Bay Vegan Restaurants & Cafes

### No Bones

**Vegan** | *Town centre (Fletcher Street)*

![](/media/restaurants/no-bones/dsc6792.webp)

![](/media/restaurants/no-bones/dsc6804.webp)

Just 100m from Main Beach, No Bones is Byron Bay's go-to spot for an all-vegan dinner with cocktails. The food is good quality overall, with a slightly fancy feel and smaller portions, so it's a great pick for a date night or a special occasion.

In my opinion, the desserts of the menu are stronger than the savoury. The tiramisu is the standout, and I think it's one of the best tiramisus I've ever had. Don't leave without ordering it.

![](/media/restaurants/no-bones/img-6424.webp)

![](/media/restaurants/no-bones/img-6426.webp)

On the savoury side, the carbonara has a good flavour overall, though I was expecting the 'vegan egg yolk' on top to be runny. The sticky tofu comes with a sauce that was a bit too bitter for my taste. The service, however, was amazing from start to finish.

> **Tip**: No Bones runs $20 Carbonara Mondays (all night, currently scheduled until 30 November 2026) and live jazz on the first Friday of every month. It's dinner only, so book ahead for weekends.

### Manna Haven

**Vegan** | *Town centre (Jonson Street)*

![](/media/restaurants/manna-haven/dsc6334.webp)

![](/media/restaurants/manna-haven/dsc6344.webp)

![](/media/restaurants/manna-haven/img-6325.webp)

![](/media/restaurants/manna-haven/img-6329.webp)

Manna Haven is a not-for-profit vegan cafe run by the Byron Bay Seventh-day Adventist Church, and it has been feeding locals for over 20 years. It's a lunch spot with a counter of hot and cold dishes that changes regularly, so you can mix and match mains, salads and desserts. It's only open for weekday lunch, so plan around it. Eat in the indoor area or the garden, or grab your food to go.

I had lunch here and tried the lasagna and the frittata. The frittata (more like a quiche) was nice and loaded with pumpkin, and the staff offered to heat it up for me. The lasagna tasted good but was a bit too salty for my taste. There's no coffee here, but there are smoothies, a house chai made on rooibos, hot chocolate and herbal teas.

### Combi

**Vegan-options** | *Town centre (Fletcher Street)*

![](/media/restaurants/combi/dsc6471.webp)

![](/media/restaurants/combi/dsc6465.webp)

Combi is one of Byron Bay's best-known brunch cafes, with an organic menu of açai bowls, smoothies, toasts and specialty coffee. There are good vegan options across the menu, and it opens at 7am every day, so it's a good breakfast stop before the beach.

I tried my friend's loaded banana bread, which was a highlight. It was toasted with crispy edges while still soft in the middle, and loaded with vegan cream and fruit. The mocha I had, however, was a bit watery.

### Frankies Gelato

**Vegetarian** | *Town centre (Fletcher Street)*

![](/media/restaurants/frankies-gelato/dsc6454.webp)

A short walk from Main Beach, Frankies is a Sicilian-inspired gelateria with 40% of the flavours are vegan, so there's always a good selection to choose from. The gelato here is creamy and delicious. I tried the matcha macadamia flavour, which had a great balance of nutty macadamia and strong matcha.

## Vegan-Friendly Grocery Stores & Takeaway

### Red Ginger

**Vegan-options** | *Town centre (Jonson Street)*

![](/media/restaurants/red-ginger/dsc6356.webp)

![](/media/restaurants/red-ginger/dsc6355.webp)

Red Ginger has been a Byron Bay favourite since 2002. It's part Asian grocery store, part dumpling house and part gift shop. It's a beautiful store to buy handmade gifts and get a vegan Asian snack.

The front of the shop has a hot food cupboard with quite a few clearly-marked vegan options. The red bean bun and soy skin spring roll were tasty. The skin on the gyoza was a bit dry, but that's to be expected when it had been sitting in the cupboard for a while. All options are reasonably priced.

### Fundies Organics

**Vegan-options** | *Town centre (Jonson Street)*

![](/media/restaurants/fundies-organics/dsc6375.webp)

Fundies is a long-running health food and organic grocery store on Byron's main street. It's a good place to stock up on vegan snacks, plant milks and organic produce for your stay.

It also has a separate garden cafe and fresh food section with vegan options like salads, burgers, brownies and more. I didn't try anything from the cafe on this trip, but it's handy if you want to grab something quick or put together a beach picnic.

## More Vegan-Friendly Spots (Not Yet Visited)

I didn't get to these two on this trip, so I can't vouch for the food yet. But they come up again and again as favourites with local vegans, and they're at the top of my list for next time.

### Vagabond

**Vegetarian** | *Arts & Industry Estate (Habitat)*

Vagabond is a plant-based cafe in the Habitat precinct, a short drive or bike ride from the town centre. It was opened in 2020 by Penny Loughnan and Anthony Baker, who co-founded the Combi brand. Everything is baked in-house with whole foods and no refined sugar, and the menu includes kitchari soup, sourdough crumpets, nourish bowls and plenty of cakes. The menu is vegetarian, so double-check that your dish is fully vegan.

**Address:** Shop 33–34, Habitat, 1 Porter St, Byron Bay | **Hours:** Mon–Sat 6:30am–3pm, Sun 6:30am–2pm | [Details](https://www.instagram.com/vagabondbyronbay/)

### Folk

**Vegetarian** | *Ewingsdale Road (edge of town)*

Folk is an organic vegetarian cafe with a garden setting on Ewingsdale Road, the main road into Byron Bay. Vegan-friendly dishes on its menu include a soba noodle salad and granola with house nut milk and hibiscus vegan yoghurt. Some dishes offer cheese or egg add-ons, so order without them. Parking is limited, and reviewers mention fines for parking outside the marked spots.

**Address:** 1/399 Ewingsdale Rd, Byron Bay | **Hours:** Breakfast and lunch, check before you go | [Website](https://folkbyronbay.com/)

## Essential Travel Tips

### Getting There

Byron Bay doesn't have its own airport. The closest is Ballina Byron Gateway Airport, about a 30-minute drive south, while Gold Coast Airport is about 45 minutes north and has more flights. Shuttle services run from both airports into town. If you're flying via the Gold Coast, check out my [Gold Coast vegan guide](/articles/vegan-gold-coast-guide) too.

### Plan Around Opening Hours

Many Byron cafes close mid-afternoon. Combi closes at 3pm, and Manna Haven is only open for weekday lunch. No Bones, on the other hand, is dinner only. So plan your day accordingly. For example, I had Manna Haven for lunch, Combi and Frankies for afternoon tea, and No Bones for dinner.

### Time Your Visit With the Markets

The Byron Community Market runs on the first Sunday of every month, and the Byron Farmers Market is every Thursday morning (8–11am) at Butler Street Reserve. From October to April, there's also a twilight market at Railway Park on Saturday evenings. Stalls change, so check vegan options on the day.

## 3 Top Sightseeing Spots

### Cape Byron Lighthouse

The lighthouse is Byron Bay's most famous landmark, sitting on the most easterly point of mainland Australia. Walk up from town along the coastal track for sweeping ocean views. Keep an eye out for whales during the migration season, roughly June to November.

![Cape Byron Lighthouse](/media/destinations/byron-bay/dsc6689.webp)

![Byron Bay beach](/media/destinations/byron-bay/dsc6517.webp)

### Main Beach & The Pass

Main Beach is right in town, about 100m from No Bones and Frankies. Walk east along the sand to The Pass, Byron's best-known surf break, to watch the surfers.

### Bangalow

This pretty village is about a 15-minute drive inland. It's home to the second Red Ginger store, and the Bangalow Market runs on the fourth Sunday of every month.
