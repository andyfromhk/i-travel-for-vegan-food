Brisbane's vegan scene has grown so much over the years. Whether you're craving ramen, Thai curries, Indonesian street food or tofu gelato, you'll find something delicious here, from fully vegan cafes to restaurants with dedicated vegan menus.

To make this guide easier to use, I've grouped my favourite places into all-vegan restaurants, cafes and bakeries, vegetarian spots that are mostly vegan, places with great vegan options, and the monthly vegan market.

---

## All-Vegan Restaurants, Cafes & Bakeries

### Vega Cafe

**Vegan** | *Spring Hill*

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/img-8304.jpeg)

*Murtabah from lunch menu*

Vega Cafe is an all-vegan cafe in Spring Hill, easily reached on the free Spring Hill Loop bus (route 30) from the city.

The food here is consistently high quality, with great attention to detail. It's hard to choose a favourite, as most dishes I've had are delicious. The murtabah is a must-try, with its fragrant curry chicken filling in a crispy roti pocket. The vegan omelette is another favourite from the breakfast menu, with its creamy, egg-like texture. For something sweet, go for the Shibuya toast, a buttered tower of toast topped with ice cream and fruit.

They also open for dinner a few days a week, and the yellow curry on the dinner menu is one of the most flavourful and rich curries I've had in a while. There's also a good selection of cabinet desserts, including croissants and doughnuts. Overall, Vega Cafe is one of the best vegan eateries in Brisbane and well worth a visit.

### Neon Ramen

**Vegan** | *Everton Park*

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/img-9038.webp)

This colourful, anime-themed ramen bar in Everton Park is a gem, serving delicious Japanese classics in seriously generous portions, especially the stir-fries.

Everything here is vegan, and the ramen and stir-fries are highly customisable: pick your sauce, noodle type and toppings. These days, I'd go for the stir-fries, like the flavourful garlic mushroom sauce stir-fry. The ramen is decent, but the broth isn't very complex in flavour. Or you can try one of their stir-fries, which is so big that it could easily feed two people.

Be sure to leave some space for dessert. I've tried all four options, and they're all superb. The ice cream is soy-based and creamy, and the purple yam doughnuts are a must-try.

### U Tong Thai

**Vegan** | *Clayfield*

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/u-20tong-20thai-2001.jpeg)

*Crispy mock honey chicken & whole coconut curry*

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/u-20tong-20thai-2002.jpeg)

*Pineapple fried rice*

This all-vegan Thai restaurant is a great choice if you're craving authentic Thai dishes. Highlights include the whole coconut curry with seafood and veggies, pineapple fried rice and pad see ew. The portions are great, and some of the curries, like the drumstick one, are big enough for two people.

They also have vegan desserts and drinks. I still recommend the mango sticky rice, but it's seasonal, so it isn't always available. For drinks, try the red Thai milk tea if you don't mind something sweet.

### The Green Edge

**Vegan** | *Windsor*

![Korean BBQ pork & seafood marinara pizzas](/media/restaurants/the-green-edge/img-5934.webp)

![](/media/restaurants/the-green-edge/img-5737.webp)

The Green Edge is the OG of the Brisbane vegan scene, and both the cafe and the shop are all vegan. It's walking distance from Windsor Station, only a few stops from the city, and there's parking available.

Stop by the shop to stock up on vegan goodies. Besides groceries, they carry makeup, household supplies, shoes, recipe books and more. The selection is impressive, though prices are understandably on the higher side.

The cafe serves pizzas, burgers, pasta and more. The food quality is good, especially the pizza range. There's also a deli section, a dessert cabinet and an ice cream parlour inside the shop if you're feeling a bit extra after your meal.

### Jing Si Books & Cafe

**Vegan** | *Salisbury*

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/img-8519.webp)

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/img-8527.webp)

Jing Si Books & Cafe is a peaceful, not-for-profit vegan cafe and bookshop tucked away in Salisbury, and it regularly hosts activities and workshops. It's a nice, comfortable space to relax.

The drinks are the highlight. They're interesting and well made, with harder-to-find options like a black sesame latte and a genmaicha latte. The pastries come from Made with Love on the Gold Coast.

The hot savoury dishes, however, are rather simple and a bit disappointing. I had the dumpling soup from the light meals menu. The dumplings were decent, but the broth was very mild and watery. I'd come here for a drink and a pastry rather than a full meal.

## Vegetarian Restaurants (Mostly Vegan)

### Vegeme

**Vegetarian** | *South Brisbane*

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/vegeme-20dishes.jpeg)

*(Top to bottom) Rice with bean sauce, sweet & sour pom pom mushroom, suji rice, satay fried rice*

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/img-0405.jpeg)

*Noodles with bean sauce is one of my favourites here*

Vegeme is an Asian fusion restaurant in South Brisbane, on the edge of West End. Most of the dishes are vegan and clearly marked, and the food is generally delicious with great portions. Standouts include the bean sauce on rice or noodles (one of my favourites, be sure to add 'beancurd wallet' to the dish to make it even more satisfying), sweet and sour pom pom mushroom rice and suji (Taiwanese fried chicken).

### Tea Master

**Vegetarian** | *Fortitude Valley*

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/img-1222-jpg.jpeg)

Tea Master is a vegetarian restaurant near the valley that is one of the OG of Brisbane's veg scene. The Asian style dishes are mostly vegan and clearly marked. You'll find bento boxes, stir fries, noodles, hot pots and more. You'll also find a wide range of drinks including vegan bubble tea, but keep in mind that they use a one-use plastic cup for bubble tea.

### Tinh Lac

**Vegetarian** | *Inala*

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/img-8511.webp)

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/img-8503.webp)

Tinh Lac is a hidden gem tucked away in an alleyway at the back of Inala Plaza. The menu features traditional Vietnamese dishes at reasonable prices, and all but one dish are either vegan or can be made vegan.

I tried the cơm tấm bì (Vietnamese broken rice). This interesting and flavourful dish combines crispy 'pork' skin, chewy vegan meat, pickles and fresh vegetables, all brought together by a sweet and tangy vegan 'fish' sauce.

> **Tip**: Be extra clear when ordering, as some staff have limited English. When I ordered, they initially forgot to make my dish vegan, though they fixed it quickly once I double-checked.

[**_See restaurant details here →_**](/restaurants/tinh-lac-vietnamese-vegetarian-restaurant)

### Botanica Real Food

**Vegetarian** | *Multiple locations*

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/img-2076.jpeg)

This upscale salad shop sells beautiful gourmet salads that are healthy and delicious. It’s a takeaway shop and there are no seats inside, but it’s not too difficult to find some benches within walking distance.

On the day of visit, the branch on Doggett St (near Fortitude Valley, or the Valley) has 5 out of 7-8 options that are vegan. There’s also a vegan chocolate brownie. Even though the vegan options are not marked clearly, the staff is knowledgeable of which ones are vegan. I've tried a few of their options are they are all great, but I love the creamy dill potato salad especially.

## Restaurants With Good Vegan Options

### Charlie's Raw Squeeze & MooFree Burgers

**Vegan-option\*** | *Multiple locations*

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/img-6935.webp)

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/img-7051.webp)

Charlie's Raw Squeeze has several shops around Brisbane and is known for its açaí bowls, smoothies and toasties. Its sister brand, MooFree Burgers, is now only available at the Greenslopes (Stones Corner) shop. There, you can get chicken and beef patty burgers with fillings like cheese, mayo and hash browns, plus loaded chips, nuggets, shakes and sundaes. Overall, the food quality is decent, with some misses from time to time.

Charlie's used to be fully vegan, but at the time of writing, the Clayfield branch has started selling non-vegan meat sandwiches, which is disappointing. As far as I know, the other branches are still vegan, but it's worth checking when you order.

### BrewDog/DogTap

**Vegan-options** | *Fortitude Valley & Murarrie*

![](/media/restaurants/brewdog-dogtap/img-8928.webp)

BrewDog has two venues in Brisbane: DogTap, its brewery in Murarrie, and a bar in Fortitude Valley. I've been to both, and on my last visit I went to DogTap, which is very spacious. Both have great vegan options, including a few pizzas, a parmy, cauliflower wings, burgers and even vegan milkshakes.

I went for their 2-for-1 pizza deal and tried the mushroom pizza and the eggplant parmy pizza, which worked out to about $13–14 each. They're decent woodfired pizzas. The base is slightly dense, but they were better than I expected from a brewery, and I'm happy they use vegan cheese. I also really loved the vegan milkshake; I got the Oreo flavour.

## Vegan Markets

### QLD Vegan Markets

**Vegan** | *Mount Gravatt*

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/dsc9283.webp)

![](/media/guides/vegan-brisbane-guide/dsc9212.webp)

If your trip lines up with it, don't miss the QLD Vegan Markets. They're held once a month at Mount Gravatt Showgrounds, usually on the first Friday evening of the month. Everything is vegan, with lots of food stalls plus vegan-related products to browse. Entry and parking are free, and it's dog-friendly too.
